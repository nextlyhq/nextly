With nextly's cache handlers, a slow lookup of the tag expiry ledger can no longer make an instance serve an entry filled before another instance's write. Each instance learns the database's clock from its lookups, and it took the statement to have run halfway through the round trip. Under load, a lookup that waited for a connection could then put an entry seconds later than it began, past the write's recorded expiry, and that entry was served for its whole lifetime. The statement is now taken to have run as late as its answer arrived, so an entry looks no later than it began, and the tightest recent reading is kept over a slower one. A slow lookup can still cost a rebuild, but never a stale read.
