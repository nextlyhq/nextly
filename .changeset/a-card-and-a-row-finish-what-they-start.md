A card drew a hairline on all four sides and left no room behind it, so its
heading and body sat flush against the border. It now has padding inside the
surface it draws.

A row of columns laid out a grid and left the gutter at zero, so the one block
whose purpose is side-by-side content rendered its columns touching. It now
spaces them by the same amount the gallery and the accordion already use, so
three grid containers do not space their children three different ways.

Both are the same shape: a block that took a visual commitment and stopped one
property short of finishing it. Neither changes what a page stores, and an
author who has already set their own padding or gap still wins, since authored
styles outrank a block default.
