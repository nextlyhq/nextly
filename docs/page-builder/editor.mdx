The editor opens with the page on the canvas, the inspector on the right for the
selected block, and a rail of named panels on the left. One panel is open at a time.
Choosing a panel's item again closes it and gives the canvas the room back.

## Where a page opens

Clicking a page in the Pages list opens it in the editor, on the page itself. So
does any entry whose collection's first shown field, after its title and address,
is a Page Builder field, a collection made in the Schema Builder included, and so do the dashboard's
Continue card, the list's search and a middle click into a new tab. A collection
with two Page Builder fields opens on the first one. Old links to a page's form
still open the form.

The rest of the page is one step away, in the top bar's **⋯** menu at its far end:

- **Open as form** opens the page's form, which holds its history, versions,
  languages, scheduling, releases and other plugins' panels. Unsaved changes go
  with you and nothing asks; the form's **Open in builder** brings you back with
  them.
- **Pin to dashboard** (or **Unpin from dashboard**) is the same pin as on the
  form, with its message and **Undo**. Your pins show in the dashboard's
  Continue card.

In the Pages list, a row's **⋯** menu offers **Edit details**, which opens the form.

The arrow at the start of the top bar reads **Back to Pages** (the list's own
name for other collections) and returns to the list, asking first if there are
unsaved changes. On an entry that opens as a form, such as a post, it reads
**Exit editor** and returns to the form.

A Single whose main field is a page layout, such as a Homepage, opens in the
builder the same way, from the sidebar and every other link. Its **⋯** menu
offers **Open as form**, and its arrow reads **Back to content** and returns to
the Content screen, asking first if there are unsaved changes.

To keep a collection's entries, or a Single, opening as a form, set
`admin: { opensIn: "form" }` on it in code. See
[Where an entry opens](/docs/plugins/admin-ui#where-an-entry-opens).

## With nothing selected

Click empty canvas space, or press <kbd>Esc</kbd> until nothing is selected, and the
inspector shows **Page settings**: the page's **Title**, its **Address** with a button
that copies it, its **Header & footer**, and the search result the page would show.
Changes there are the same fields **Settings** edits: the title in the top bar follows
as you type, and they're saved with the page when you save. **All page settings…**
opens **Settings** on the page's own tab, for every other field. The inspector keeps
its width, so the canvas doesn't move as you select and deselect.

The search result is built from the page's title and address. Where the SEO plugin
sets search and sharing for the page's type, the result is left to it.

**Page data** says how much data the page loads each time someone opens it, against
the site's limit: "Page data: 12 of 500". A list's entries, an image, a link to an
entry and a related entry each count once. Past the limit, the blocks that need more
come up empty on the live site. Choose the line to see what the page loads and, over
the limit, which blocks come up empty; choose one of those to select it.

The top bar shows the same count only once the page nears its limit, from 80% of it:
"Page data: 430 of 500 · what's this?" in amber, and in red with "· over the limit"
once the limit is used up or passed. If the count can't be made, it says "Page data:
couldn't count · Retry", since a page whose count is hidden could be over its limit.

The limit is 500 unless the site's code sets another with
`pageBuilder({ maxQueries })`, a whole number up to 100,000. The page routes use it too, so the editor and the live
page count against one number. A route given its own `maxQueries` uses that instead,
and the editor doesn't know it. `Infinity` sets no limit, and the top bar then never
shows the count.

A component, a saved section and a collection template show their own settings in
the same place: **Component settings**, **Saved section settings** and **Collection
template settings**. A component's lists its fields and variants.

## The top bar

The top bar runs across the editor in three parts.

| Part   | What it holds                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Left   | The way back to the entry, the page's title (choose it to switch to a recent page or to all pages), whether it's a draft or published, and **Undo** and **Redo**. |
| Centre | The devices to edit and preview the page at, and the zoom. The device you choose is the breakpoint your style changes go to.                                      |
| Right  | The buttons that hide or show the left panel and the inspector, **Search**, whether your work is saved, **Preview** and its menu, and **Publish**.                |

**Preview** shows this version of the page, where the admin offers a preview. With
unsaved work, it saves a draft first (never publishing), so the preview shows what the
canvas shows; if the save fails, the preview doesn't open and the error says why. If you
can't save the page, it shows the saved version instead. Its arrow opens a menu:
**Preview this version** does the same, and says "Saves a draft first" while there is
unsaved work; **Preview as visitor** hides the blocks whose conditions don't match, on
the canvas ([Previewing as a visitor](#previewing-as-a-visitor), below).

**Publish** is the entry's own leading action: **Publish**, **Publish changes** or
**Save**, as on the entry's page. Its arrow holds **Save draft**, **Schedule publish…**
and **Unpublish**, each where the entry offers it. An action you can't use right now is
greyed out and says why. Duplicating, discarding and deleting stay on the entry's page.

### Bindings to fix

When a field, a Single or a block changes so a binding on the page no longer fits, a
chip beside the page's status says how many: **2 bindings to fix**. Choose it for the
list: each binding's block and prop, what changed, and what visitors see meanwhile (its
fallback, the block's own value, or nothing). **Select** selects the block, opens Layers
on it and brings it into view; **All bindings to fix** opens the site-wide list. The chip
shows only on a page that has one.

The site-wide list is **Bindings to fix**, under **Checks** in the Page Builder's panel
(`/admin/plugins/nextlyhq-plugin-page-builder/bindings-to-fix`), for people who can edit
pages. The Page Builder overview leads with it while any need fixing. It lists every document whose bindings no longer fit, by place (pages, components,
saved sections, header & footer sets, other entries and Singles), each with **Open in editor** at its block.
The dashboard's **Needs your attention** card counts them and links to it, and a
**Bindings to fix** card can be added under **Customize**.

A binding in a page's header & footer set region opens the component that region places, where it is
edited. One in a language other than the default has no link, since the editor edits the
default language. A document's draft is checked as well, so a binding broken only in a
draft is listed before that draft is published. Until the first check of every document
finishes, the list says it hasn't checked yet rather than that every binding fits.

### Where a Single's values are shown

A Single's side panel has a **Shown on (from the builder)** section. It lists each page,
header & footer set and component where the builder shows one of the Single's values,
with the block it's in: **Home, Hero section**. Each row opens that document in the editor
with the block selected. A block takes its name from Layers: the name you gave it, or its
block's name.

- A binding inside a component is listed once, on the component, not on every page that
  places it.
- Bindings in a draft count, so a binding you haven't published yet is listed.
- You see only the places you're allowed to open. The rest are counted: **and 2 you can't
  open**.
- The section shows 10 rows. **and N more** opens the full list.
- The list knows only documents made with the builder. Code that reads the Single
  directly isn't listed, and the section says so.

The list comes from an index of every builder document's bindings to Singles. It's kept
by the same check as **Bindings to fix**, on every save and whenever a field or a block
changes. After you upgrade and run `nextly migrate`, the section says **Still checking
pages…** until the first check of every document finishes. That takes a jobs pass or two.

### Publishing a page whose parts aren't published

A page, the components it places and its header & footer set are published separately. When you
publish a page that places a component that isn't published, or one placed inside
another component, or names a header & footer set that isn't published, the editor tells you before
it publishes. The dialog lists each part, and what visitors see meanwhile: nothing where
an unpublished component sits, and the site's default header & footer set around the page until its
own is published.

- **Publish page only** publishes the page and leaves the parts as they are. It's the
  first choice, because publishing a page before its parts is an ordinary order of work.
- **Publish both** (or **Publish all**) publishes the ticked parts first, then the page.
  It's offered only for parts you may publish yourself, and each is published as you.
  A part you can't publish is listed with the reason, and stays unpublished.
- **Cancel** or Escape publishes nothing.

The same question is asked when you publish a page or a component from its own entry
page. A component you publish from its own view is checked the same way, for the
components it places.

The save state reads **Saving…**, **Unsaved changes**, **Saved**, or **Not saved** with
a **Retry** button. It covers the page and the site-wide styles you change in **Design**.

Undo and Redo take the same steps as the keyboard. A text edit still open on the canvas
is finished first, so its words are the step undone.

A change to the site's shared styles in **Design** (a variable, a class, a font or a
breakpoint) is a step of the same history. Those are saved as you make them, so undoing
one saves the style as it was before, and the page's own save state doesn't change.
These saves don't check for changes made since you opened the editor yet: when two people
change a variable, a class or a font at the same time, the later save wins. A breakpoint change
is the exception: if someone else's change is stored while yours is being saved, yours is
refused instead of replacing it.

Hiding a side gives the canvas its room. The left panel you had open comes back when
you use the rail again. The editor remembers which sides you hid, for you.

The device buttons show Desktop and the site's two widest breakpoints. A site with more
puts the rest in a menu beside them, under **More devices**. While you style one of those,
the menu's button shows its name and width. The buttons never change places.

When the bar is narrow, the device buttons drop their label first and keep their icons.

## From the entry's form

On an entry's form, the Page Builder field is a card with a small picture of the page,
drawn inside its header and footer with its images, as the canvas draws it. Under the
picture, **Sections (N)** is folded; open it to list the page's sections, and choose one
to open the editor at that section. **Open in builder** opens the editor at the top.
Choosing a section in the picture does the same as choosing it in the list.

On a new entry that has never been saved, the card offers **Save draft and open
builder** (**Save and open builder** where the collection keeps no drafts). It saves the
entry as the form's Save does, with the same checks, then opens the editor. If a
required field is empty, you stay on the form and the errors show there.

## A field inside a group or a repeater's row

A page's blocks field doesn't have to sit at the top of the entry. One inside a group
opens at its path, `?field=hero.body`. One inside a repeater's row opens for that row,
and the address names the row by its saved id rather than its place in the list,
`?field=sections.<rowId>.body`, so it opens the same row after the rows are reordered.

The top bar names where the field sits beside the page's title, such as
**Sections · Hero banner · Body**, and so does the start of the bottom bar. A row is
named by its title, as the entry's row header shows it, and by its place only when it
has no title. While the editor is open, the entry's fields panel leaves out the repeater
holding the row, so its rows can't be reordered or removed under the editor. Leaving
returns you to the entry with that row's **Open in builder** focused.

- **Save the entry once first.** A row gets its id when it is saved. The button of a row
  added since the last save is greyed out and says "Save the entry once to design this
  row."
- **Rows open in the default language.** The Page Builder edits the default language's
  rows, and a translated repeater can hold different rows in each language. So while the
  entry shows another language, a row's button is greyed out and asks you to switch to
  the default language.
- **A row that's gone opens nothing.** An address naming a row that has since been
  removed, or naming a row by its position (`sections.1.body`), isn't opened on
  another row: the editor says the row can't be opened and offers the way back.
- **Saving goes to that row.** The save writes into the row with that id, wherever it
  now stands, and leaves the other rows as they are. If the row is no longer in the
  entry when you save, the save is refused and nothing is written into another row.
- **A repeater whose rows set their own `id` field** gives its rows no saved id, so its
  rows don't open in the editor. Their button says why, and the server log names each
  such field at startup.
- **Some locks aren't enforced on save inside a row yet.** The server can't read a
  row's saved layout to compare against. So a save of a row's layout isn't checked
  against Layers locks or variant locks, and a content-only slot is compared with its
  starting blocks alone. The server log notes each save whose Layers locks it couldn't
  check. Layers locks inside a group aren't checked on save either.

## The inspector

The inspector edits the selected block in up to three tabs, in this order:

| Tab      | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Content  | What the block says, shows or links to, and its structure: a Button's label and link, a Heading's text.                                                                                                                   |
| Style    | How the block looks.                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Advanced | Where and when it shows (**Hide on**, **Show when**, and **Keep out of search previews** on every container), then its HTML: its **HTML tag** where it has a choice, **Anchor (ID)**, **CSS classes** and **Attributes**. |

A tab with nothing in it for the selected block isn't shown: a Spacer or a Section,
which say nothing themselves, have only Style and Advanced. The tab you choose stays
chosen as you select other blocks. A block without it opens on its first tab, and the
next block that has it opens on yours again. If the editor opens with a state such as
Hover already chosen, it opens on Style.

**Style** starts with **Block style** on a block whose type has saved styles: a Section's
Plain and Hero, a Button's Primary, Secondary, Outline and Text, an Image's Square,
Rounded and Circle. Each is a picture tile drawn in your site's own colours and fonts.
The block's own style is marked **Default**, and the one the block wears is ticked. Pick
a tile to change the block's style. A block style is shared by every block of the type
that wears it, so the line under the tiles says so ("A saved style for every Section.").
**Edit block styles** there opens **Design**, **Styles**, at that type's block styles,
where you change, add, rename or remove them. A change made in the block panel goes to
the selected block only, on top of the style it wears.

Under it, or at the top for a block with no saved styles, one line says what a change
will affect:

- **● This block** (pink), or **◆** and the name of a shared style (green) when you
  chose to edit that style instead;
- the device the canvas shows, such as **Desktop**. You choose the device in the top
  bar; hover the chip to see which devices a change reaches;
- the state, **Normal** or another such as **Hover** (amber).

Open the first chip to choose between this block and each shared style it uses, to
**Detach** or **Remove** one, or to **Apply a shared style…** or **Save as shared
style…**. These items are always there, in the same order: one you can't use yet is
greyed out with the reason under it. While a shared style is chosen, a line under the
chips says where it's used and that changes there change all of them. While a state
other than Normal is chosen, the settings below are tinted and say which look you're
editing. This line and the tabs stay in view as you scroll the panel.

In Content, a link reads the same on every block that has one: **Link**, then **Open in
a new tab** once a link is set. A Heading links to a page, an entry or an address, as a
Button does. A List's **List as** picks Bullets or Numbers, and **Start at** shows only
for Numbers. A custom list marker is a CSS detail: add it from **+ Add CSS property** in
Style. In Style, a Button and every container have a **Shadow** under Effects, and a
Heading and a List have a **Background**.

In Advanced:

- **Show when** reads "Always shown" until you add a condition. Inside a Collection
  list it also names whose fields the conditions read, such as "Each post in the list".
- **Keep out of search previews** marks the block's element `data-nosnippet`, so Google
  doesn't quote its text under the page's link or in AI Overviews. Google reads the mark
  on `<section>` and `<div>` only, and the panel says so when the tag is another one.
- **HTML tag** lists each tag once, with a plain name and what it means: "Section — a
  themed part of the page `<section>`".
- **Link relationship** (`rel`, such as `nofollow`) on a block that links, and a
  Button's **Button type** (Button, Send form or Clear form), offered while the button
  links nowhere. An Embed's **Allow full screen** is here too.
- **Anchor (ID)** lets a link jump to the block: `#your-id`.
- **CSS classes** takes names separated by spaces, added to the block's element beside
  the classes Nextly generates. A name that starts with a digit, `.`, `#` or `nx-`, or
  holds a character a class can't, is refused under the field with the reason, and the
  other names are kept.
- **+ Add attribute** adds a `data-`, `aria-` or other open attribute. A class goes in
  CSS classes, not here.

Each tab is a list of sections divided by lines. Click a section's name to fold it or open
it; any number can be open at once. A dot beside a section's name means it holds a value
for the block you're editing. Sections stay as you left them, for every block and the
next time you open the editor. A section you've never folded opens if it holds a value or
is the tab's first. **Expand all** and **Collapse all** in the panel's **⋯** menu fold
every section of the tab you're on.

Every control says what it writes. Hover it, or reach it with the keyboard, and a tooltip
shows the CSS or HTML it sets: `min-height: 120px`, `<section>`, `id`. The tooltip stays
while your pointer is over it, and Escape closes it. If you'd rather always see them,
turn on **Show CSS names** in the **⋯** menu: each control then prints its CSS property
names in small type under its label. The editor remembers the choice in this browser.

**Find a setting**, under the tabs, finds a setting by its name, the CSS it writes or
another common word for it: "colour" or "color", "z-index", "corners" for the corner
radius, "nofollow" for Link relationship. The tab you're on shows only what matches, each
under its section with the section opened, and each tab's label says how many it holds.
When the tab you're on has none, it says so and links to the tabs that do ("1 in
Content"). Press <kbd>/</kbd> anywhere in the panel outside a text field to jump to the
field, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> or ✕ to clear it; your sections come back as you left them. The
search stays while you select other blocks.

**Only changed**, beside it, shows only what the block holds a value for: in Content and
Advanced, settings you've set; in Style, values at the device and state you're editing.
A line says where else the block holds styles ("Also changed on Tablet (2) · Hover (1)"),
and each part of it switches to that device or state. With both on, a setting must meet
both.

The Style tab lists its sections in the same order for every block: Block style, Layout,
Inside its parent, Spacing, Size, Position, Text, Background, Border, Effects, Icon and
CSS properties, each shown only where the block offers it. Advanced lists Visibility, then
HTML.

Each section shows the settings people reach for first. Everything else the block can use
is in **CSS properties**, the last section, by its CSS name. **+ Add CSS property** opens a
list you can search, of only the properties that work on this block as it is now: a block
laid out as a plain stack isn't offered the flex or grid ones, and **z-index** is offered
once the block is positioned or sits in a row or grid. Each entry shows its CSS name, its
plain name and the section it belongs to. Pick one and its row appears with its value
ready to type. A row stays while it holds a value, and its **✕** (**Remove z-index**, for
example) clears the value and removes the row in one step that **Undo** brings back. When
everything that applies is already there, the list says **Nothing left that applies
here.** "Find a setting" finds these too: a property you haven't added is listed under CSS
properties with **Add**.

**Background** picks an image rather than taking an address: **Choose** opens the Media
library, **Upload** adds a file, or paste a link into the field below them. A thumbnail then
shows with **Replace** and **Remove**; Remove clears only the image. **Position** places the
image on one of nine points (hover a point to see the CSS it writes); a position set any
other way reads **Custom** with its value. **Size** is **Cover** (fills the block, cropping
the edges), **Fit** (the whole image, once) or **Tile** (the image at its own size,
repeated). Each choice sets the repeat with the size, so Fit never tiles, and one undo takes
both back; any other size can be written as CSS from **…** and reads **Custom**.

**Animation when it appears**, in Effects, gives a block an entrance: **None** (the
default), **Fade in**, **Rise**, **Slide in from the left**, **Slide in from the right** or
**Zoom in**. **Duration** and **Delay** are in milliseconds (600 and 0 when you leave them
empty), and **Start** is **When scrolled into view** (the default) or **When the page
loads**. Like any style, it's set per device: a block can rise on desktop and stay still on
mobile with **None** there. Scrolled into view, the block moves in as it enters the window,
following the scroll, and a delay holds it back for part of that. The published page
animates with CSS alone and adds no script, and only the block's opacity and position move,
so nothing around it shifts. The published page shows blocks where they end for a visitor
whose device asks for reduced motion, for a printed page, and, for **When scrolled into
view**, in a browser that can't run scroll-driven animation. The canvas shows them where
they end too, except that it plays a block's animation once when you change it, and again
when you press **Play** beside the setting; under reduced motion it plays nothing. A page
using a slide clips what sticks out past its sides.

## Search

**Search** in the top bar, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> on a
Mac), finds four kinds of thing as you type, each under its own heading:

- **Add**: blocks and your components to add. Each says where it will go, as the Add panel
  does ("Adding inside Hero, at the end"), and choosing one adds it there, by the same
  rule as a click in the Add panel (see [Where a clicked block lands](#where-a-clicked-block-lands)).
- **On this page**: the page's blocks, by the names Layers shows, with what each is and
  what it sits in. Choosing one selects it and scrolls it into view.
- **Pages**: the site's other pages you may open, by title, with **Draft** beside a page
  not yet published. Choosing one opens it in the editor.
- **Commands**: what you can do to the selected block, Undo and Redo, and closing the
  editor.

The bottom line says what <kbd>Enter</kbd> does to the highlighted result. Saved sections
are added from the Add panel's **Sections** tab.

The rail shows only the panels that work in this editor. A panel that is not built yet
has no item at all, so nothing on the rail opens an empty panel.

| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Add**      | The blocks, components and saved sections you can place. Drag one onto the canvas, or choose it to place it by the selected block (see [Where a clicked block lands](#where-a-clicked-block-lands)), or at the end of the page when nothing is selected.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Layers**   | The page as a tree. Select, expand, move, hide, lock and rename blocks here. See [Working with a block's row in Layers](#working-with-a-blocks-row-in-layers).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Pages**    | The site's pages, with search. Open, add, duplicate, rename or delete one without leaving the editor. See [Pages](#pages).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Design**   | The site's look, in three tabs: **Variables** (named colours, type sizes and spacing, each with a light and a dark value), **Shared styles** and **Fonts** (see [Fonts](#fonts)). A variable's dark value applies when a visitor's device prefers dark. The Variables tab imports and exports DTCG files, including one file per mode for Figma. A variable that a page, a component, a shared style or a block's default style still uses can't be removed: change those uses first. Add, Layers and Design sit at the top. Settings and Help sit at the bottom.

The editor remembers the panel you left open, and on Design the tab you left on, the
next time you open it. It remembers them for you, not for the browser, so two people
who share a browser each find their own.

## Where a clicked block lands

Choosing a block in the Add panel (a click, or <kbd>Enter</kbd> on a tile), the
Components panel or search places it by what is selected and what you add:

- **Nothing selected**: at the end of the page, above the footer.
- **A block that holds blocks is selected** (a Section, Box, Columns, Card, Accordion,
  Gallery…):
  - a content block (Heading, Text, Image, Button…) goes **inside it, at the end**;
  - a layout block (Box, Columns, Column, Card) goes **after it**, so Box then Box gives
    two Boxes one after the other, not one inside the other.
- **A block that holds no blocks is selected** (a Heading, say): anything goes after it.
- **A Section or a saved section** goes after the top-level block that holds the
  selection, never inside another section.
- **A component** is placed as a layout block when one of its top-level blocks is one,
  and as a content block otherwise.

If the place the rule picks can't take the block (Columns hold only Column blocks, so a
Heading can't go inside them), it goes after the selected block instead, then after each
block around it, out to the page. A Column added with Columns selected goes inside them,
since nothing after them takes it. A container's own **+** adds inside that container,
whatever you add.

The line at the top of the Add panel says where the next block goes before you choose
it: "Adding inside Hero, at the end", "Adding after Heading, inside Hero" or "Adding at
the end of the page". Each tile tells a screen reader where it goes, and after an insert
a screen reader hears what was added and where ("Heading added inside Hero, at the
end"). The new block is selected, so the next one goes after it.

### Columns ask for a layout

A new **Columns** row shows "Choose a layout" on the canvas in place of its two empty
columns, however you added it: **2 equal**, **3 equal**, **4 equal**, **Wide left**,
**Wide right** and **Wide middle**, the same tiles as in the row's Content tab, and
**Skip**, which keeps two equal columns. Move between the tiles with the arrow keys,
pick with <kbd>Enter</kbd> or skip with <kbd>Escape</kbd>. The choice is part of adding
the row, so one Undo removes the whole row. Clicking elsewhere also keeps two equal
columns, and the question is never asked again for that row, even after a reload.

## Adding a block where you are

Press <kbd>/</kbd> on the canvas, with a block selected or nothing selected, and a small
picker opens beside the block (or at the end of the page), so you can add a block
without going to the Add panel. It holds:

- **Search blocks**, with the cursor in it. Typing lists the blocks, saved sections and
  components that can go there, from the same list and words as the Add panel.
- **The six blocks you add most**, in a grid. Until you have added any they are Heading,
  Text, Image, Button, Box and Columns. They are counted for you in this browser, and a
  block that can't go there is skipped for the next one.
- **Browse all blocks and sections →**, which opens the Add panel instead.

A line at the top says where the block will go, by the same rule as the Add panel's
(see [Where a clicked block lands](#where-a-clicked-block-lands)). Arrow keys move
through the blocks, <kbd>Enter</kbd> adds the highlighted one, and <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves
to **Browse all**. The new block is selected; a block with text, such as a Heading,
opens for typing, and anything else leaves you on the canvas. <kbd>Escape</kbd> or a
click outside closes the picker without adding anything and puts you back where you
were.

While you are typing in a block's text, <kbd>/</kbd> is just a character.

## Adding an image

A new Image starts empty: on the canvas it is a box, 16:9 unless you set its own
proportion, reading **Add an image** with three ways to fill it.

- **Upload** opens your computer's file chooser. The picture goes into the media
  library, with the same checks as the library's own upload, and its progress shows on
  the block.
- **Media library** opens the library to choose a picture that is already there. A click
  anywhere on a picture's tile chooses it, and a double-click chooses it and closes the
  library.
- **Link** takes the address of a picture elsewhere. It is kept only once it loads as a
  picture; otherwise the block says **This link didn't load an image**, with **Try
  another link**, and stays empty.

You can also drop a picture file on the box, or on an Image that already has a picture
to replace it. A file that isn't a picture is refused with **That file isn't an image**,
and nothing changes. If an Image's file is deleted from the media library, the block says
**This image's file is missing**, with **Replace**.

An Image with no picture draws nothing on the published page.

### An image's settings

With an Image selected, its **Content** tab holds:

- **Image**: the picture, with **Replace** to choose another.
- **Alt text**: what screen readers say for the picture. A picture from the media
  library brings the alt text the library holds for it: the field shows those words,
  marked **From the media library**. Type your own to replace them for this page;
  empty the field, or choose **Use the media library's alt text**, to follow the
  library's again. Tick **Decorative, no alt text** for a picture that adds nothing to
  the page's words: screen readers skip it, and the page's `img` carries `alt=""`.
- **When clicked**: **Nothing**, **Link** or **Enlarge**. Only **Link** shows the link's
  settings: where it goes (an address, or a page or entry), opening in a new tab, and
  its relationship. A link you set stays stored when you choose another option, so
  choosing **Link** again brings it back. An Image saved before this setting existed
  reads as **Link** when it has a link, and **Nothing** otherwise. On the live page,
  **Enlarge** makes the picture a button that opens it full size over the page, with
  its caption. **Close**, <kbd>Escape</kbd> or a click outside the picture closes it,
  and it works with JavaScript turned off. The full-size file loads only when the
  picture is opened. On the canvas, a click on the picture still selects the block.
- **Caption**: shown under the picture, as the figure's caption.

The shapes **Square**, **Rounded** and **Circle** are block styles, at the top of the
**Style** tab. **Circle** crops the picture to a square first, so a wide picture is a
circle, never an oval.

## Bold, italic and links in a Text

A Text is one paragraph of words, edited where it is shown: double-click it on the
canvas, or select it and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Select some words and a small bar
appears just above them:

- **B** makes them bold and **I** italic. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>B</kbd> and
  <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>I</kbd> do the same.
- **Link** (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd>) asks for the address, with **Open in a new
  tab**. An address that would run a script is refused. With a link selected, **Remove
  link** takes it off.

<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>F10</kbd> moves to the bar from the keyboard, the arrow keys
move along it, and <kbd>Escape</kbd> goes back to the words.

A Text stays one paragraph: <kbd>Enter</kbd> (and <kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd>)
starts a new line in it. Paste a passage with headings or lists and the words stay
while the rest goes, with the note "Text keeps words, bold, italic and links. Use Rich
text for headings and lists". Headings, lists and quotes belong in a **Rich text**
block.

The Text's field in the inspector edits the same words, and the canvas shows them as you
type. A Text bound to a field takes a text field's words as one plain paragraph; a
rich-text field isn't offered for it.

## Pages

**Pages** in the rail lists the site's pages, grouped by the kind of page when the
site has more than one, with a search. A page shows **Draft**, **Changed** (published,
with changes not yet published) or **Scheduled** beside its title when it isn't
simply published, and the page you're editing is marked **Editing**.

Choose a page to open it in the editor. Each page's **⋯** menu offers:

- **Open**.
- **Rename**, in place: <kbd>Enter</kbd> keeps the new title, <kbd>Escape</kbd> leaves
  it. Renaming a published page is a change like any other, published with the page.
  For the page you're editing, **Rename** opens its Settings, where its title is.
- **Duplicate**, which makes a draft copy, "Copy of …", and opens it. A draft, or a
  page with changes not yet published, can't be duplicated yet: publish it first.
- **Settings**, which opens that page's Settings.
- **Delete…**, which asks first, naming the page. The page you're editing can't be
  deleted from here: open another page first.

**New page** asks for a title, then opens the new draft.

The page title in the top bar is a switcher too: it lists the pages you opened most
recently, in this browser, and **All pages…** opens the Pages panel.

Opening another page with work not yet saved asks first, as leaving the editor does.

## Fonts

**Design → Fonts** lists the fonts on this site, each drawn in itself, and the site's
font variables. Choose a font to see its styles and where it came from, or to remove it.
Removing a font keeps its files in your media library.

Fonts are stored on your site. Visitors' browsers never contact Google.

**From Google Fonts.** Choose **+**, then **From Google Fonts**. Search, filter by
category, or type your own preview text. Choose a family, then its weights (a variable
family comes as one file covering every weight), italics and languages, and **Add**.
Your server downloads the files into your media library, in a **Fonts** folder, and the
published page loads them from your site only.

While you browse, the editor's browser loads previews from the catalogue's file host,
`cdn.jsdelivr.net` by default. Only the editor contacts it. The list of families and
every file you add are fetched by your server, from `api.fontsource.org` and
`cdn.jsdelivr.net`. If your admin sends a Content-Security-Policy, allow
`font-src https://cdn.jsdelivr.net` for the previews.

**Upload a font file.** Choose **+**, then **Upload a font file**: a `.woff2` or `.woff`
file, its family, weight and style, and a confirmation that you may use it on the web.

**In the inspector**, Style → Text → **Font** offers only the site's font
variables, the fonts on this site and the system fonts. **Add or manage fonts…** opens
this tab.

Only a role allowed to update the site style can add or remove a font. Others see the
tab read-only.

The catalogue's hosts and the limits on one add are set on the plugin:

```ts
pageBuilder({
  fonts: {
    // The defaults. `catalogue: false` turns the catalogue off; uploads still work.
    catalogue: {
      api: "https://api.fontsource.org",
      files: "https://cdn.jsdelivr.net",
    },
    // Each may be lowered, not raised.
    limits: {
      filesPerAdd: 24,
      bytesPerFile: 2 * 1024 * 1024,
      bytesPerAdd: 10 * 1024 * 1024,
      timeoutMs: 15_000,
    },
  },
});
```

## Spacing

Blocks have no outer margin of their own. The space between two blocks is the gap of
the container holding them, set per container or by the spacing variables:

- **Section, Box, Card, Column**, an accordion item and a Collection list put a gap above
  every block after the first. Set the container to flex or grid and the same gap
  becomes its `gap`.
- **Columns** and **Gallery** space their columns and images with a wider gap.
- **Rich text** spaces its own paragraphs, headings and lists, inside the block.
- **Section** pads its content above, below and beside it. **Card** pads its content.
  Box and Column have no padding.

Every container also says how it arranges its blocks, under **Style → Layout →
Arrange**: **Stack**, **Row** or **Grid**, with the CSS it writes shown small under the
choice ("display: flex · flex-direction: row"), and the same CSS on hover.

- **Stack** puts blocks one under another. It writes nothing until you set its
  **Align**, **Space between** or **Reverse order**, which make it a flex column.
- **Row** puts blocks side by side. **Stack on** stacks it on a device and the smaller
  ones; it still reads as a Row there, and its **Align**, **Spread** and **Reverse order**
  then go by the column. **Spread** (Packed, Space between, Space around, Even) shares
  out the room along the row, and while it isn't Packed the 3×3 **Align** says "Spread is
  on" for that axis. **Wrap onto new lines** lets blocks run onto a new line.
- **Grid** sets **Columns**, a number per device or **As many as fit** a minimum width,
  and **Rows**. **Set rows and columns apart** gives each its own space.

The 3×3 **Align** names its nine points, from Top left to Bottom right; with none chosen,
blocks fill the width. Values that stop applying after a change are kept, and the
section says so ("2 settings from Row aren't used · Clear"); **Clear** removes them in one
step you can undo. A layout set in CSS with no plain equivalent, such as `inline-flex`,
reads **Arrange: Custom** with the CSS rows that hold it; **Use plain controls** rewrites
it to the nearest plain choice.

Every block with a size says how it takes its place in its parent, under **Style → Inside
its parent**: **Width: Fill**, **Fit** or **Fixed**, and **Height** the same when the
parent arranges as a Row or a Grid. Fill takes the space it's given, Fit is as wide as its
content, and Fixed is a width you set. Each choice writes what it means for the parent's
arrangement at the device you're editing: in a Row, Fill takes the rest of the row. In a
Row stacked on that device, a block is sized as in a Stack and Height isn't offered there.
Values that match no choice read as **Custom**, with the CSS rows that hold them.

Every container has **Content width** under **Style → Layout**: **Boxed** or **Full
width**. A new Section starts Boxed, and every other container starts Full width.

A Boxed Section is a full-width band: its background reaches both edges of the page, and
only its content is held to the site's content width (`content.width`), with at least
`space.gutter` at each side. Box, Columns, Column, Card, Accordion, Gallery, Collection
list and Entry content work the same way when Boxed. Padding you set at a Boxed block's
sides takes the gutter's place: more moves the content further in, and 0 removes the
gutter, but the content never runs wider than the content width. A Boxed block you give
its own width (under **Size**) is centred at that width instead. Full width lets the
content run edge to edge.

The line under the buttons says what is in force: "Boxed to 1152px · site default".
**Change for this section** gives that one container a width of its own, typed (`1400px`,
`90%`) or picked from the site's variables. It applies to that container only: a Boxed
block inside it still boxes to the site's width. **Use site default** takes it away.
Content width is one setting for every device and state, so it is held while you edit a
shared style or a state other than Normal.

Each value is a variable under **Design → Variables**, and most grow with the screen:

| Variable           | What it sets                                 |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| `space.stack`      | The gap between blocks in a container        |
| `space.section-y`  | A section's padding above and below          |
| `space.gutter`     | A section's padding at the sides             |
| `space.columns`    | The gap between columns and gallery images   |
| `space.card`       | A card's padding                             |
| `space.paragraph`  | The space between paragraphs of rich text    |
| `button.padding-y` | A button's padding above and below its label |
| `button.padding-x` | A button's padding at the sides of its label |

Spacing you set on a block (its margin, padding or gap in **Style → Spacing**, or
through a shared style) always wins over these defaults on that block. A margin above
a block meets its container's gap in one of two ways, depending on the container's
layout:

- In normal flow, the block's own top margin **replaces** the gap: a 40px margin
  under a 20px gap leaves 40px.
- In flex or grid, the gap is the container's `gap`, and a margin on a block **adds**
  to it, as margins do in CSS: the same block sits 60px below its neighbour. To change
  the spacing of a flex or grid container, set its **Space between** rather than a
  margin on each block.

A block hidden on a device still counts as a block above the next one. When every
block above it is hidden, the first block shown in a normal-flow container keeps the
gap above it; give that block a top margin of 0 on that device to remove it.

### Setting margin and padding

**Style → Spacing** draws a block's margin and padding as one box: margin is the outer
ring and padding the inner one. Each side shows its value.

- **Click a side** (or press Enter on it) to open its popover. It holds:
  - your spacing variables as presets, smallest first, after **None**;
  - a number with its unit;
  - **Apply to**: **This side**, **Top & bottom** (or **Left & right**), or **All sides**.
- **Type a number** on a side to set it straight away. Tab saves it and moves to the next
  side, so `4 0 Tab 4 0 Tab…` goes round the box: Top, Right, Bottom, Left, margin first,
  then padding.
- **A value in green** comes from one of your spacing variables, so it changes when the
  variable does.
- **To reset a side**, use the popover's **⋯ → Reset**, right-click the side, or press
  Alt+Backspace (Option+Delete on a Mac).

With a block selected, the canvas shows the space above it from its container as
**Gap · from Section** (or the container's name), not as the block's own margin.

### Using a variable in a field

Every other value in **Style** reaches your variables through its own menu:

- **A size** (a font size, a gap, a radius, a width): open its unit menu. Your variables
  come first, under **Your spacing** and **Your sizes**, then the units.
- **A colour**: open its swatch. Your colour variables come first.
- **A font**: open the font list. **Font variables** come first.
- **A field with no unit menu** (a line height, a shadow) has a menu of its own at its end.

A field holding a variable shows it in green with the variable's name. Hover it, or move
to it with Tab, to see what it is, for example "Spacing variable XL · 64px ·
--site-space-xl": its kind, its value and the CSS name the page reads it from. Click the
name to choose another variable. **Unlink** keeps the value the field shows now as a plain
one, so it no longer changes with the variable. To remove the value, use the row's reset.

## The bottom bar

The bottom bar shows where you are: **Page**, then each block holding the selected
one, ending with the selected block. Choose any of them to select it; choose **Page**
to clear the selection.

On its right, a hint names the keys that work from there, such as

<kbd>Enter</kbd> to edit the selected block's text, <kbd>Escape</kbd> to go up
to the block holding it, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>K</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd>+
<kbd>K</kbd> on a Mac) for the command palette.

## Settings

**Settings** at the bottom of the rail opens a dialog with two tabs.

**This page** holds the entry's own fields, such as its title and address, as the
entry's page draws them. Where a plugin adds a tab of its own, such as the
[SEO plugin](/docs/plugins/seo#in-the-page-builder), these sit under **General**, with
the plugin's tab beside them. They save with the page, from **Save** or **Publish** in the top
bar. Below them, a preview shows roughly how a link to the page looks when it is shared.
It uses the SEO fields where the page has them, then the page's first text and image, and
the page's title. A heading never sets the title. Your site's own metadata may differ. Pressing Enter in a field
never saves the page.

**Site** applies to the live site as soon as you change it. You see it only if your role
may manage the page builder's site settings.

- **Breakpoints** lists the site's devices, in the same words as the device buttons.
  **Edit breakpoints** opens the editor in place. **Save breakpoints** stores the whole
  set at once, after checking it, and **Cancel** leaves it as it was. If you close
  Settings with an edit open, it asks before throwing the edit away.
- **Site default header & footer** names the header & footer set pages without one of their own
  are shown in. **Change in Pages ▸ Header & footer** opens the list of sets in a new tab, where
  **Make site default** chooses another (see
  [The default header & footer set](/docs/page-builder/layouts#the-default-header--footer-set)).

The site's breakpoints change only here. A write to the site style that changes them
any other way, through the API or the site style's own form, is refused.

### A plugin's tab in Settings

A plugin adds a tab to the Settings dialog at the Page Builder's admin place
`nextlyhq-plugin-page-builder.settings`, which takes tabs (`@experimental`). Name the Page
Builder in your plugin's `dependsOn`, or in `optionalDependsOn` so a site without it still
starts, with your tab left out:

```ts
contributes: {
  admin: {
    contributions: [
      {
        id: "acme-notes.builder-tab",
        kind: "tab",
        place: "nextlyhq-plugin-page-builder.settings",
        label: "Notes",
        component: "@acme/notes/admin#NotesTab",
      },
    ],
  },
},
```

The tab is drawn for a page or a design, never for a header & footer set or a component. Besides
`setBadge`, it receives:

- `context`: `userId`, `document` (`"page"` or `"template"`), the edited document's
  `collection` and `entryId` (null before its first save), and `draws`, for a design the
  collection whose entries it draws and the one entry an entry's own design draws.
- `seo`: the document's own metadata (`settings.seo`) as `value`, and `set(next)`, which
  writes it whole, or removes it with `null`, as one edit the editor's Undo takes back.
- `design`, the document as it is being edited; `control`, the entry's form; and
  `writable`.

## Moving blocks in Layers

Drag a row in Layers to move its block. A line shows where it will land. Drag the row
to the right to put the block inside the block above it, and to the left to take it
out. Rest on a closed block for a moment and it opens, so you can drop inside it. A
drop the block is not allowed to make shows a red line and says why, and letting go
leaves the block where it was. Press <kbd>Escape</kbd> to cancel a drag. Dragging is off
while a search filters the list.

Every move also works without dragging: select the block and press

<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>↑</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>↓</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd>+
<kbd>→</kbd> or
<kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>←</kbd>, or right-click it on the canvas and choose **Move
up**, **Move down**, **Move in** or **Move out**.

## Working with a block's row in Layers

- **Point at a row** and its block is outlined on the canvas. **Click a row** to select
  its block; the canvas scrolls to it.
- **Hide a block on one device.** Point at a row and click its eye: the block is hidden
  on the device you are editing, on the published page. Every other device keeps
  showing it. A hidden row is dimmed and says **Hidden**, and a row hidden on another
  device shows an eye with a slash through it, its tooltip naming the devices. On the
  canvas, at a device it is hidden on, the block is still drawn, striped and tagged
  "Hidden on Mobile · still here in the builder", so you can find and edit it; a
  visitor on that device sees nothing of it, and Preview as visitor shows the page
  without it.
- **Hide on**, in the Advanced tab's Visibility section, has one toggle per device the
  site defines, each with its icon and name. Press a device to hide the block there
  alone: hiding it on Desktop leaves Tablet and Mobile showing. Hover a toggle to see
  the CSS it writes, such as `display: none at 768–1023px`. While the block is hidden
  anywhere, every tab says so at the top ("Hidden on Mobile. Visitors on that device
  won't see it."), with **Change** to go to Hide on.
- **Lock a block** with the padlock on its row. A locked block can't be moved, changed or
  deleted, nor can anything inside it, until it is unlocked.
- **Rename a block** by double-clicking its row, pressing <kbd>F2</kbd>, or choosing
  **Rename** from the row's menu. <kbd>Enter</kbd> keeps the name and <kbd>Escape</kbd>
  keeps the old one. An empty name goes back to the block's type. The name is for you:
  it never appears on the published page.
- **Right-click a row** for its menu: Rename, hide or show on the device, Lock or
  Unlock, and the block's other actions.
- **Search** filters the rows, keeping the blocks around each match. Arrow keys move
  through the rows, <kbd>→</kbd> and <kbd>←</kbd> open and close a block, and
  <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to the first and last row.

## Binding a prop to a field

A block's prop can show a field's value instead of its own: the entry's (a page's own,
or the entry a template is previewed with), a Single's, or the site settings'. In the
Content tab, a prop that a field can fill has a bind button beside it. Choose it for
the fields that fit, grouped by where they come from, with each one's value as you read
it. A field visitors can't read says **Not public**, and one whose value is only in a
draft says **Draft**.

Once bound, the prop shows a chip naming the field and where it comes from, with its
**Fallback** under it: what visitors see when the field is empty or they can't read it.
The chip's menu has:

- **Change field** opens the fields again.
- **Open source** opens the screen the field is edited on in a new tab: the entry's,
  the Single's, or the site settings. It isn't offered for a list's item, which is a
  different entry on every row.
- **Unbind** keeps what visitors saw in the prop, and a note under it says what was
  kept and why. A field visitors can't read keeps the fallback instead.

**Format.** A date or a number shows as text only through a format, so a text prop bound
to one has a **Format** under its fallback. The picker names the format a date or a number
starts with, **Long date** or **Number**, and a pick writes it with the binding; change it
there. Dates offer a long or short date, the weekday and date, the date and time, the
time, or the month and year, and only those the field can show: a field holding a day
offers no time, and one holding a time offers no date. Numbers offer a plain or whole
number, a percent, a compact number (1.2K), or a currency, whose three-letter code you
type beside it. A format written through the API that the page can't use is shown in
red with the reason.

**Images.** An image's bind button is on its **Source** row, and binds the picture to a
media field or to a URL field holding an image address. The image address field, on the
URL side, and the alt text each have a bind button of their own. A bound image's
**Fallback** is an image address, shown with a small picture of it: one starting
`https://` or `http://`, or a path on the site such as `/media/spring.jpg`. Anything else
is held back with the reason.

A picture bound to a media field is described by its file's own alt text. Alt text you
type replaces it for this page, and **Use the media library's alt text** goes back to
the file's. A picture bound to a URL field carries no alt text, so there is none to go
back to.

The fallback can also be an image from the media library. **Choose from library**, the
button at the end of the Fallback row, opens the newest images with a search by name:
use the arrow keys to move through them and Enter to pick, or click one. **Browse all…**
opens the full media library, where you can also upload. An image address pasted into
the search is offered as **Use this address**. A chosen image is shown by its picture
and file name, with a button to remove it, and the canvas's "Visitors won't see this"
badge names it the same way.

When an image is unbound and visitors saw it by its
address (the field held one, or they saw the fallback), the address is kept in
**Image address** and the image is left empty, since an image chosen from the library
holds a library file only. The note under the Source row says so.

Inside a link, an image or a list, the parts that qualify the main value (how a link
opens, whether an image is decorative, a list's query) are set in their own controls and
have no bind button.

## Editing a template

A template draws every entry of the collection it names, so while you edit one the
canvas draws it with a real entry of that collection. The **Previewing** strip pinned
above the canvas names that entry in full, with its status: Draft, Published, or Changed
(published, with saved changes not yet published). It stays in place while the page
scrolls, and appears only on a template.

- **The entry it opens on** is the one you last previewed this template with in this
  browser session, or the most recently updated entry you may read.
- **Choose another** from the strip's chip. The list shows the entries you may read,
  drafts included, most recently updated first, with when each was updated. Each shows
  its saved title, and one with changes saved but not yet published is marked Changed.
  Type to search the saved titles. Choosing an entry previews it as you would read it,
  its unpublished changes included, so the chip and the canvas show its newest title.
- **An entry's own design** previews the one entry it serves. There is nothing to
  choose, so its chip opens no list and the keys below don't apply.
- **The keys** <kbd>Shift+Alt+↓</kbd> and <kbd>Shift+Alt+↑</kbd> preview the next
  and the previous entry in the list, from the canvas or the panels but not while you
  type. A short message says which entry you switched to.
- **New** opens the screen for a new entry in a new tab and leaves the template open.
  When you come back to the editor the list is read again, so the new entry is there to
  choose. It is offered only if you may create entries in that collection.

Bound text and lists of related entries on the canvas read the entry you preview as you
would read it, drafts included. Visibility conditions are checked against what a visitor
reads instead, when you apply them (below). When there is no entry you may
read, the chip says so, for example "No posts to preview", and the canvas draws each
binding's fallback until there is one. The words are the same whether the collection is
empty or you may read none of it. If the entry you chose can't be read when the editor
asks for it, for example because it was deleted, the chip says so and offers to try
again, rather than naming an entry the canvas isn't drawing.

Which entry you preview is not saved with the template. When the template is saved, its
lists and conditions are checked against the collection it draws.

### Previewing as a visitor

While the canvas draws every block, a block with visibility conditions carries a
**Conditional** badge, whoever the page would show it to. To see the page as a visitor
gets it, open the arrow beside **Preview** in the top bar and choose **Preview as
visitor**. On a template, it checks the conditions against the entry being previewed:

- **A block a visitor would see** is drawn as usual, with the badge **Shown for this
  post** (named after the collection's entries).
- **A block a visitor wouldn't see** is drawn as one dashed line in its place, naming
  it and why: "Members' worksheet · hidden for this post: Category is not Guides". It
  stays selectable, so you can change its conditions.
- **A strip above the canvas** reads "Previewing as a visitor", then what the
  conditions did: "1 block hidden for this post", or "Every conditioned block shows for
  this post" ("Every block checked shows" while the template also holds blocks it doesn't
  check, below). On a template it sits under the **Previewing** strip. **Stop** turns
  the visitor's view off, as choosing the menu item again does; the item shows a tick
  while it's on.

The conditions are checked against the entry as a visitor reads it: its published values
and the fields visitors may read, not your unpublished changes. An entry that was never
published hides every conditioned block, and its line says so. A condition that can't be
checked, for example on a field visitors can't see, hides its block from every visitor,
and its line says so, with a warning sign.

A block inside a list's entries is checked against each entry the list draws, as the
published page checks it. While you preview as a visitor, the canvas's lists show what a
visitor would see: published entries only, with only the fields visitors may read, not
the drafts you can see. An entry that hides the block draws its line in the block's
place, saying why for that entry: "Premium badge · hidden for this entry: Price isn't
more than 10". A list that shows a visitor fewer entries than you see says so above its
entries: "Showing what visitors see: 4 of 6 entries". Stop to see your own entries
again, drafts included. The strip's count is of the page's own blocks, and says
"Conditions checked for each entry in the list" when a list's blocks are all there is to
check, adding "some blocks aren't checked" when a header & footer set region also holds conditions.

Blocks inside a placed component are checked with the page's: a component's own block
whose conditions don't hold is drawn as its line where it would be, "Members offer ·
hidden for this page: Title doesn't contain Members", and the strip counts it, once for
each place the component is drawn. A press on the line selects the placement, as a press
on any of the component's blocks does.

Blocks inside a region of the page's header & footer set aren't checked yet: they keep their
**Conditional** badge while you preview as a visitor, and the strip says some blocks
aren't checked.

Blocks inside the entry's own content, drawn by **Entry content** (below), are checked
with the template's: a hidden one is drawn as its line inside Entry content, and the
strip counts it.

On a page, the conditions read the page's own fields. The page is checked as a visitor
reads it, its published values and the fields visitors may read: "1 block hidden for
this page" says what the conditions did. A page that was never published hides every
conditioned block.

**Preview as visitor** is always in the menu, but it can't be turned on everywhere. It
is greyed out, with the reason under it:

- "No block on this page has conditions", when nothing on the page, its placed
  components included, has a condition;
- "No entry to check here. The published page checks them where it's placed.", when
  you edit a component or a header & footer set's header or footer: the published page checks them
  where it is placed;
- "Needs an entry to check its conditions against.", on a template with no entry to
  preview, or on a Single, which is no entry of a collection.

The visitor's view is yours for this session: nothing is saved, and it is off again
when you reload the editor.

### Showing the entry's own content

An entry can hold blocks of its own, such as a post's Body. To draw them inside the
template, add **Entry content**, under **Entry** in the Add panel. It is offered only
while a template is open, and a template holds one: with one placed, its tile says
"A template holds one Entry content block.", and Duplicate is turned off for it.

- **Field** chooses which of the collection's blocks fields it shows, from the fields
  stored on the entry itself. When the collection has one, it is chosen for you; when it
  has several, the first is chosen and you can pick another.
- **On the canvas** it shows the entry you preview, drafts included, read-only: a click
  anywhere inside selects Entry content itself, and the content is edited on the entry.
  **Open post** (named after the collection) opens that entry in a new tab. A faint
  dashed outline with a tab, such as "Entry content · Body", marks the block whether or
  not it is selected.
- **When there is nothing to show** the canvas says why: "The post’s Body shows here."
  with no entry to preview, "_Title_ has no Body content yet." for an empty field, and,
  in red, "The Body field no longer exists in Posts, so this shows nothing. Choose
  another field." when the field was removed from the collection. A template saved
  before its field was removed keeps saving, and Field shows the old choice as
  "Body (removed)" until you choose another.
- **On the published page** it shows the field as visitors may read it. When they may
  not read it, or it is empty or gone, nothing is drawn: no markup, and none of the
  field's content in the page.

Entry content is refused when it is saved anywhere but a template, an entry's own blocks
field included, and so is a second one, or a field the collection doesn't have as a
blocks field. A blocks field inside a repeater is stored on each row, not on the entry,
so it is never offered. **Save as section…**, **Update a saved section** and **Turn into
component** are turned off, with the reason, while the selection holds Entry content.

## Help

Help lists the keyboard shortcuts the editor has right now, read from the same list the
keyboard uses. So a key shows there only if pressing it does what Help says. When the
editor and the page around it both bind a key, Help shows the binding that runs inside
the editor.

Escape closes Help and returns you to the rail.
