title: Audit Log
description: A durable record of who changed what, and when.
---

The audit log records mutations as they happen: which user, which action, which document,
and which fields changed.

## Availability

| | |
|---|---|
| Built in | No plugin required, and not gated behind a paid tier |
| Admin | Activity is surfaced on the dashboard |
| Permission | Reading activity is an admin capability |

## What a record holds

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Actor | Who made the change |
| Action | What happened — a create, an update, a delete |
| Target | The collection and document |
| Changed fields | Which fields differed, computed from the write itself |

<Callout type="warn">
**System writes are not recorded.** A mutation made by internal or system context, such as a
seed or a migration, produces no activity entry. If you rely on this log, that gap is the
thing to know: the absence of an entry does not mean nothing happened.
</Callout>

Recording the changed field list — rather than the whole document — keeps the log useful
without turning it into a second copy of your content. [Version
history](/docs/versions) is where the full before-and-after lives.

## Erasing a person without erasing the record

A deletion request under privacy law asks you to remove a person's data. An audit log
exists precisely so records cannot quietly disappear. Those pull in opposite directions.

Nextly resolves it by separating the actor from the act: a person's identifying details can
be erased while the entries they produced remain, still counted, still ordered, still
showing that a change happened and what it touched. You keep the record and lose the
person, which is what both obligations actually require.

## Signed-in people a plugin defines

A plugin can sign in people who are not staff, such as a shop's customers (see
[Signed-in people](/docs/plugins/signed-in-people)). What such a person does appears in the
security audit trail, not in the activity log above: the activity log records staff users only.

A row about such a person names them by kind and id, in fields of their own. It records which
kind of caller acted (`staff`, `apiKey`, `core`, `anonymous`, or the person's kind, such as
`accounts:account`), and it keeps its network address, browser and the plugin's metadata, as
it does for a staff user who exists.

When the plugin reports the person deleted, Nextly does the following in one transaction:

- erases the address and browser on every row the person acted on;
- erases the plugin's metadata on every plugin row about them;
- replaces their id with a keyed digest of their kind and id.

It then keeps only that digest and the time, never the id, so every row written about the
person afterwards is written already erased. Rows about anyone else stay as they were, a
staff user who happens to share the person's id included, and so does the address of a staff
member who acted on the person.

The digest's key is a random key Nextly makes once and keeps sealed under `NEXTLY_SECRET`.
After you rotate the secret, keep the old one in `NEXTLY_SECRET_PREVIOUS` until the app has
started once with both: the key is sealed again under the new secret at that boot, and then
the old secret can go. If the old secret is dropped before that, the key cannot be read:
writes naming a person and deletion reports fail, and each failure is logged.

## Retention

Audit entries are pruned on a schedule, so the log holds a useful window rather than
growing forever.
